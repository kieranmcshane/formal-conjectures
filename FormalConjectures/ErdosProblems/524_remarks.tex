\begin{abstract}
This memo accompanies the Lean~4 formalization of Chojecki's (2026)
resolution of Erdős Problem~524 — the almost-sure order of magnitude of the
supremum on $[-1,1]$ of a random algebraic polynomial with Rademacher
coefficients. It records
(a) the mathematical architecture of the proof as formalized,
(b) precise statements of the sorry-free closed pieces,
(c) the atomic set of axioms used to stand in for Mathlib-level
prerequisites (Brownian motion, Itô integral, Gao–Li–Wellner, 2D KMT,
endpoint reparametrization), and
(d) mathematical subtleties and open concerns uncovered in the course of
formalization. The memo is updated as the branch progresses. Cross-references
\texttt{typeset in this font} point to actual Lean names in
\texttt{FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem and its resolution}

\subsection{Erdős's original question and the Salem–Zygmund clarification}

Let $t \in (0,1)$ have binary expansion $t = \sum_{k\ge 1} \eps_k(t) 2^{-k}$
with $\eps_k(t) \in \{0,1\}$, and set $a_k(t) := (-1)^{\eps_k(t)} \in
\{-1,+1\}$. Under Lebesgue measure on $(0,1)$ the sequence $(a_k)_{k\ge 1}$
is i.i.d.\ Rademacher. The associated random algebraic polynomial is
\[
  P_n(t)(x) := \sum_{k=1}^n a_k(t)\, x^k,
\]
and Erdős asked for the correct order of magnitude of
\[
  \supnorm_n(t) := \max_{x\in[-1,1]} |P_n(t)(x)|.
\]
(Per Salem–Zygmund~1954 the supremum is taken over $x \in [-1,1]$ with
Rademacher $\pm 1$ coefficients; Erdős's original statement used binary
digits $\{0,1\}$ and $x \in [0,1]$, which is equivalent modulo
reparametrization.)

\subsection{Chojecki's theorem (January 2026)}

Chojecki's paper establishes two complementary results.

\begin{theorem}[Upper envelope, Chojecki~2026]\label{thm:UE}
Almost surely,
\[
  \limsupop_{n\to\infty}\, \frac{\supnorm_n(\omega)}{\sqrt{2n\log\log n}}
  = 1.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Sparse lower envelope, Chojecki~2026 Theorem~18]\label{thm:SLE}
Let $n_m := \lfloor e^{m^3}\rfloor$. There exist explicit constants
$\alpha_\pm = (1/(6c_\mp))^{1/3}$, where $c_- \le c_+$ are the
Gao–Li–Wellner small-deviation constants for the centered Gaussian
process $Y(u) = \int_0^1 e^{-us}\,dB(s)$, $u \ge 0$, such that almost
surely
\[
  \alpha_- \le \limsupop_{m\to\infty}
  \frac{\log\!\big(\sqrt{n_m}/\supnorm_{n_m}(\omega)\big)}
       {(\log\log n_m)^{1/3}} \le \alpha_+ \,.
\]
Equivalently, $\supnorm_{n_m}(\omega) = \sqrt{n_m}\cdot
\exp(-\Theta((\log\log n_m)^{1/3}))$ infinitely often.
\end{theorem}

The \emph{exact} asymptotic constant $\alpha_*$ in
$\lim_m \log(\sqrt{n_m}/\supnorm_{n_m})/(\log\log n_m)^{1/3} = \alpha_*$
is open (Remark~19, Chojecki); it would require the as-yet-unestablished
sharp one-point small-ball asymptotic for $Y$.

\subsection{Six helper modules}

Under \texttt{FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/Helpers/} we build:

\begin{description}[labelwidth=4cm,leftmargin=4.5cm,style=nextline]
  \item[\texttt{StirlingTwoSided}] Effective two-sided Stirling bound
    with absolute constants (no Robbins error for the upper direction,

  \item[\texttt{IndepSetBridge}] Bridge lemma:

  \item[\texttt{CentralBinomLower}] Sharp pointwise lower bound on the

\section{The LIL backbone (sorries \#2, \#3, \#4)}

The upper envelope (Theorem~\ref{thm:UE}) reduces to a functional LIL for
the simple symmetric random walk
\[
  S_n(\omega) := \sum_{k=1}^n a_k(\omega),
  \qquad \phi(n) := \sqrt{2n\log\log n}.
\]
Classical Kolmogorov's LIL states
$\limsup_{n\to\infty} S_n / \phi(n) = 1\ \as$ Chojecki's paper uses this
as a building block; Mathlib lacks the $\limsup\ge 1$ direction, so we
formalize it from first principles.

\subsection{Sharp Rademacher lower tail at the LIL scale (sorry~\#2)}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{lil\_tail\_lower\_at\_scale}, closed]\label{thm:tail}
For every $\delta \in (0,1)$ there exist $N(\delta) \in \N$ and
$C(\delta) > 0$ such that for all $n \ge N(\delta)$ and every i.i.d.\
Rademacher sequence $a$ on any probability space,
\[
  \PP\!\left(S_n \ge (1-\delta)\phi(n)\right)
  \ge C(\delta)\cdot (\log n)^{-((1-\delta)^2 + \delta)}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The $+\delta$ slack in the exponent makes the tails summable along
geometric subsequences $n_k = \lfloor c^k\rfloor$ only for $c$ large
enough, which is exactly what the second Borel–Cantelli needs.

\paragraph{Proof architecture.} The PMF of $S_n$ is
\[
  \PP(S_n = 2k-n) = \binom{n}{k} 2^{-n}, \qquad 0 \le k \le n,
\]
formalized as \texttt{walk\_pmf\_binomial} via the cylinder-event
expansion of $\{a_j = \sigma_j\}_j$ for $\sigma \in \{\pm 1\}^n$ with
$\sum \sigma_j = 2k-n$. Writing $d := k - n/2$, a second-order Taylor
expansion of $y \mapsto (1+y)\log(1+y) + (1-y)\log(1-y)$ yields the
\emph{sharp} Kullback–Leibler bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KL-sharp}
  k\,\log\!\tfrac{2k}{n} + (n-k)\,\log\!\tfrac{2(n-k)}{n}
    \le \frac{(k-(n-k))^2}{2n}\cdot\left(1 + O\!\left(\tfrac{d^2}{n^2}\right)\right)
    = \frac{2d^2}{n}\cdot(1+o(1)).
\end{equation}
Combined with two-sided Stirling, this produces the pointwise
PMF estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PMF-sharp}
  \binom{n}{k} 2^{-n} \ge \frac{c}{\sqrt n}\,\exp\!\left(-(2+\eps)\frac{d^2}{n}\right)
  \quad\text{whenever } d^2 \le n\log n,\ n \ge N(\eps).
\end{equation}
Summing over a window $k \in [k_\star, k_\star+W]$ with
$k_\star = \lceil(n+t)/2\rceil+2$, $W = \lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$,
$t = (1-\delta)\phi(n)$, gives, for every $\omega \in
\{S_n\ge t\}$ included via $\{S_n = 2k-n\}$ with $2k-n\ge t$:
\[
  \PP(S_n \ge t) \ge W\cdot \frac{c}{\sqrt n}\cdot
    \exp\!\left(-(2+\eps)\!\max_{k\in[k_\star,k_\star+W]}\!\frac{d_k^2}{n}\right).
\]
The worst-case exponent expands as
$(t/2+\sqrt n+2)^2 = t^2/4 + t(\sqrt n+2) + (\sqrt n+2)^2$, and dividing
by $n$ and invoking the algebraic core
\[
  (2+\eps/4)\!\cdot\!(t/2+\sqrt n+2)^2/n
    \le ((1-\delta)^2 + \delta)\log\log n
\]
(encapsulated as \texttt{Erdos524.Helpers.exponent\_window\_bound\_uv})
produces the claim.

\begin{remark}[Why $\alpha=4$ is provably insufficient]\label{rem:alpha4}
If \eqref{eq:KL-sharp} is replaced by the loose first-order bound
$\log x \le x-1$, the PMF exponent degrades from $2 + \eps$ to $4$. The
resulting lower bound on $\PP(S_n\ge t)$ then produces exponent
$2(1-\delta)^2\log\log n$, which \emph{exceeds} the target
$(1-\delta)^2+\delta$ whenever $2(1-\delta)^2 > (1-\delta)^2+\delta$,
i.e.\ $(1-\delta)^2 > \delta$, i.e.\ $\delta < (3-\sqrt 5)/2 \approx
0.382$. For these $\delta$ the $+\delta$ slack cannot absorb the
factor-of-2 loss. The second-order refinement of the KL bound is
therefore \emph{necessary}, not cosmetic. In Lean, the key step that
unblocks it is keeping $u = \sqrt{2s}$ as a hypothesis $u^2 = 2s$ so that
the core inequality (\texttt{margin\_absorbs\_sqrt\_core}) is polynomial
in $(u,s,\delta)$ — three earlier agent attempts blew the
\texttt{maxHeartbeats} budget working directly with \texttt{Real.sqrt}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Block Borel–Cantelli (sorry~\#3)}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{lil\_sparse\_lower\_bc}, closed]
For $\delta \in (0,1)$ and $c > 1$, almost surely
\[
  S_{\lfloor c^{k+1}\rfloor} - S_{\lfloor c^k\rfloor}
    \ge (1-\delta)\phi(\lfloor c^{k+1}\rfloor - \lfloor c^k\rfloor)
  \quad\text{infinitely often.}
\]
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Proof architecture.} By shift-invariance of the Rademacher
sequence, the increment over the block $(\lfloor c^k\rfloor,
\lfloor c^{k+1}\rfloor]$ has the same law as $S_{m_k}$ with
$m_k = \lfloor c^{k+1}\rfloor - \lfloor c^k\rfloor$. Theorem~\ref{thm:tail}
(applied uniformly to \emph{every} Rademacher sequence by the strengthened
signature $\exists N\,C,\forall a\ldots$) gives
\[
  \PP(E_k) \ge C\cdot (\log m_k)^{-((1-\delta)^2+\delta)}
  \sim C\cdot (k\log c)^{-((1-\delta)^2+\delta)},
\]
which is not summable since $(1-\delta)^2 + \delta < 1$ for
$\delta \in (0,1)$. Disjointness of the index blocks
gives \texttt{iIndepFun} of the block sums via
\texttt{Erdos524.Helpers.iIndepFun\_block\_sums}; passing to preimage
events via \texttt{iIndepSet\_preimage\_of\_iIndepFun} yields
\texttt{iIndepSet} of $(E_k)$, and the second Borel–Cantelli
(\texttt{ProbabilityTheory.measure\_limsup\_eq\_one}) gives the
conclusion.

\subsection{Kolmogorov's LIL lower bound (sorry~\#4)}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{kolmogorov\_lil\_lower\_bound}, closed]
For every i.i.d.\ Rademacher sequence,
\[
  \limsupop_{n\to\infty} S_n / \phi(n) \ge 1 \quad \as
\]
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Proof.} Suffices to show: for every integer $m\ge 1$,
$\limsup S_n/\phi(n) \ge 1 - 1/m$ almost surely; countable intersection
gives $\limsup \ge 1$ via $\lim_{m\to\infty}(1-1/m)$.

Fix $m$, set $c := 4m^2$, $\delta := 1/(8m)$. By the block BC:
a.s.\ infinitely often,
\[
  S_{\lfloor c^{k+1}\rfloor} - S_{\lfloor c^k\rfloor}
    \ge (1-\delta)\phi(\lfloor c^{k+1}\rfloor - \lfloor c^k\rfloor).
\]
Apply Kolmogorov's LIL \emph{upper} bound (already in the file,
from Chojecki's companion argument) to $-a$, yielding a.s.\
eventually
\[
  S_{\lfloor c^k\rfloor} \ge -(1+\delta)\,\phi(\lfloor c^k\rfloor).
\]
Therefore a.s.\ infinitely often,
\[
  \frac{S_{\lfloor c^{k+1}\rfloor}}{\phi(\lfloor c^{k+1}\rfloor)}
    \ge
    (1-\delta)\cdot
      \frac{\phi(\lfloor c^{k+1}\rfloor - \lfloor c^k\rfloor)}
           {\phi(\lfloor c^{k+1}\rfloor)}
    \ -\ (1+\delta)\cdot
      \frac{\phi(\lfloor c^k\rfloor)}{\phi(\lfloor c^{k+1}\rfloor)}.
\]
The ratios converge (\texttt{Erdos524.Helpers.lilNormAux\_block\_ratio\_tendsto}
and \texttt{...\_scale\_ratio\_tendsto}) to
\[
  \sqrt{(c-1)/c} = \sqrt{1 - 1/(4m^2)}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  1/\sqrt c = 1/(2m),
\]
respectively. The limit of the right-hand side is therefore
\[
  (1-\tfrac1{8m})\sqrt{1 - \tfrac1{4m^2}} - (1+\tfrac1{8m})\cdot\tfrac{1}{2m}.
\]
A small algebraic calculation (closed in Lean by \texttt{nlinarith} on the
identity $((1-\delta)\sqrt{1-1/c} - (1+\delta)/\sqrt c - (1-1/m))\cdot 32m^3
= 12m^2 - 10m + 1 \ge 3 > 0$) shows this exceeds $1-1/m$, yielding the
conclusion.

\section{The sparse lower envelope (sorry~\#5)}

Theorem~\ref{thm:SLE} is the nontrivial content of Chojecki's paper. The
Lean formalization reduces it to six \emph{atomic sub-axioms} documenting
distinct Mathlib-level gaps, plus an \emph{assembly theorem} that
combines them; the assembly is still a \texttt{sorry}, but with all
ingredients isolated.

\subsection{Atomic sub-axioms}

The following are declared in \texttt{524.lean} immediately after the
\texttt{GaoLiWellnerConstants} structure. Each one stands for a specific
mathematical ingredient missing from Mathlib as of v4.27.0.

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=3cm]
  \item[\texttt{wiener\_process\_exists}] A standard Brownian motion
    $B = (B_s)_{s\ge 0}$ exists on some probability space with the
    usual properties. Mathlib has partial Wiener-measure infrastructure
    via \texttt{MeasureTheory.WienerSpace}; the full construction of a
    continuous-path Brownian motion is ongoing work.
  \item[\texttt{ito\_integral\_exp\_kernel}] The Itô integral
    $Y(u) := \int_0^1 e^{-us}\,dB(s)$ exists for every $u\ge 0$ and
    defines a centered continuous Gaussian process with explicit
    covariance $\E[Y(u)Y(v)] = (1 - e^{-(u+v)})/(u+v)$. Mathlib lacks
    the general Itô integral; this is a multi-year project.
  \item[\texttt{gao\_li\_wellner\_small\_ball\_upper}] The
    upper small-ball asymptotic
    $\PP(\sup_{u\ge 0} |Y(u)|\le\eps) \le \exp(-c_-|\log\eps|^3)$,
    where $c_-$ is a fixed constant.
  \item[\texttt{gao\_li\_wellner\_small\_ball\_lower}] The matching
    lower bound
    $\PP(\sup_{u\ge 0} |Y(u)|\le\eps) \ge \exp(-c_+|\log\eps|^3)$.
    Both GLW bounds require Karhunen–Loève expansion + entropy
    methods on the Gaussian process $Y$; again not in Mathlib.
  \item[\texttt{two\_dim\_KMT\_coupling}] The 2D
    Komlós–Major–Tusnády strong invariance coupling: on the same
    probability space as $B$, there exist two processes $Z_n^\pm(u)$
    coinciding in law with the reparametrized polynomials, such that
    \[\sup_{u\ge 0}\bigl|Z_n^\pm(u) - Y^\pm(u)\bigr| = O(\log n / \sqrt n)\ \as,\]
    with $Y^\pm$ two independent copies of $Y$. 1D KMT itself is an
    open Mathlib target.
  \item[\texttt{endpoint\_reparametrization}] A \emph{deterministic}
    identity: the substitution $x = \pm e^{-u/n}$ sends
    $x \in [-1,1]\setminus\{0\}$ to $u\in[0,\infty)$ bijectively on each
    sign branch, and
    \[\frac{\supnorm_n(\omega)}{\sqrt n}
      = \max\!\left(\sup_{u\ge 0}\!\left|\tfrac{1}{\sqrt n}\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^n a_k(\omega)e^{-uk/n}\right|,\
         \sup_{u\ge 0}\!\left|\tfrac{1}{\sqrt n}\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^n a_k(\omega)(-e^{-u/n})^k\right|\right).\]
    This is elementary calculus; a partial Lean proof exists in
    \texttt{Erdos524/EndpointReparametrization.lean} (modulo a few
    cascading \texttt{Real.sqrt}/\texttt{biSup} rewrites still to be
    unstuck).
\end{description}

For the Borel–Cantelli assembly we also retain two \emph{transferred}
axioms stating the small-ball bounds directly for $\supnorm_n$ after
applying endpoint reparametrization $+$ KMT $+$ GLW:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{polynomial\_sup\_small\_ball\_upper}:
    $\PP(\supnorm_n \le \eps\sqrt n) \le \exp(-c_+|\log\eps|^3)$,
  \item \texttt{polynomial\_sup\_small\_ball\_lower}:
    $\PP(\supnorm_n \le \eps\sqrt n) \ge \exp(-c_-|\log\eps|^3)$.
\end{itemize}
(See Section~\ref{sec:subtleties} Remark~\ref{rem:convention} on a naming
ambiguity in these axioms.)

\subsection{Assembly of Theorem~\ref{thm:SLE}}\label{sec:assembly}

The assembly theorem
\texttt{chojecki\_sparse\_lower\_envelope\_proof} (currently
\texttt{sorry}) takes as input the transferred small-ball axioms and
produces the two bounds $\alpha_- \le \limsup R_m \le \alpha_+$ via
Borel–Cantelli on the cubic subsequence. The key cubic-asymptotic
inputs, proved sorry-free in
\texttt{CubicSubseqAsymptotics.lean}, are:
\begin{align}
  \log n_m / m^3 &\to 1, & \log n_m &\ge m^3/2\text{ eventually},\label{eq:cubic-log}\\
  \log\log n_m / (3\log m) &\to 1, & \log\log n_m &\ge \log m\text{ eventually}.\label{eq:cubic-loglog}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Upper direction: $\limsup R_m \le \alpha_+$ a.s.}
For $\beta > \alpha_+ = (1/(6c_-))^{1/3}$, Borel–Cantelli~I gives
$\PP(R_m \ge \beta\ \text{i.o.}) = 0$ provided $\sum_m \PP(R_m\ge\beta)<\infty$.
Setting $\eps_m := \exp(-\beta(\log\log n_m)^{1/3})$,
\[
  \PP(R_m \ge \beta)
    = \PP(\supnorm_{n_m} \le \eps_m\sqrt{n_m})
    \le \exp(-c_+\beta^3\log\log n_m)
    \sim m^{-3c_+\beta^3}.
\]
Summability requires $3c_+ \beta^3 > 1$, i.e.\
$\beta^3 > 1/(3c_+)$. The claim $\beta^3 > 1/(6c_-)$ is stronger, hence
the \emph{structural} hypothesis $c_+ \ge 2c_-$ (i.e.\
\texttt{two\_lower\_le\_upper}) which we have added to the
\texttt{GaoLiWellnerConstants} structure. Under this hypothesis,
$\beta^3 > 1/(6c_-) \ge 1/(3c_+)$ and summability follows from
\eqref{eq:cubic-loglog}.

\begin{remark}[The factor of $6$ beyond $2c_+ \le c_-$]\label{rem:factor6}
The true Gao–Li–Wellner bound has $c_+ \sim c_-$ asymptotically
(they coincide up to a factor of $2$ arising from the endpoint split,
not an additional factor). Chojecki's exact factor $6$ arises as
$6 = 3\cdot 2$: the $3$ from \eqref{eq:cubic-loglog}, and the $2$ from
the identity
\[
  \PP(\supnorm_n \le \eps\sqrt n)
    = \PP\bigl(\supnorm_n^+\le\eps\sqrt n,\ \supnorm_n^-\le\eps\sqrt n\bigr)
    \approx \PP(\supnorm_n^+ \le \eps\sqrt n)^2
\]
under the (asymptotic) independence of $\supnorm_n^\pm$. The last
approximation is enforced by the KMT coupling to two independent
copies of $Y$ and is \emph{not} a consequence of the transferred
one-sided axioms as currently stated. Our structural hypothesis
$c_+\ge 2c_-$ is the minimal weakening that keeps the downstream BC1
calculation tight; a sharper formalization would either state
``product-of-marginals'' small-ball bounds as a separate axiom, or
axiomatize the KMT step at the level of coupling to \emph{independent}
$Y^\pm$ and derive the factor of~2 internally.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Lower direction: $\liminf \limsup R_m \ge \alpha_-$ a.s.}
For $\alpha < \alpha_- = (1/(6c_+))^{1/3}$, Borel–Cantelli~II requires
$\sum_m \PP(R_m\ge\alpha) = \infty$ \emph{and} mutual (or pairwise)
independence of the events. The transferred small-ball lower bound
gives
\[
  \PP(R_m \ge \alpha) \ge \exp(-c_-\alpha^3\log\log n_m) \sim m^{-3c_-\alpha^3},
\]
so divergence holds for $\alpha < (1/(3c_-))^{1/3}$ which is $\ge \alpha_-$
once the factor-of-$2$ independence is established (Remark~\ref{rem:factor6}).

\emph{The independence step is the main obstruction currently in the
Lean proof}; see Remark~\ref{rem:nested} below.

\section{Subtleties and open concerns}\label{sec:subtleties}

\begin{remark}[Naming convention of GLW constants]\label{rem:convention}
The \texttt{GaoLiWellnerConstants} structure declares two real fields
\texttt{lower, upper} with the original docstring
\[
  \exp(-\text{\texttt{upper}}\cdot|\log\eps|^3)\ \le\ \PP(\sup|Y|\le\eps)\ \le\
  \exp(-\text{\texttt{lower}}\cdot|\log\eps|^3).
\]
In this convention \texttt{upper} is the \emph{larger} constant and
drives the \emph{lower} bound on $\PP$; conversely \texttt{lower} is the
smaller constant and drives the upper bound.

The currently declared transferred axioms
\texttt{polynomial\_sup\_small\_ball\_\{upper,lower\}} invert this
convention: the ``upper'' axiom uses \texttt{glw.upper} in an upper
bound on $\PP$, and vice versa. For the default instance
$(\texttt{lower},\texttt{upper}) = (1,2)$ with the freshly added
\texttt{two\_lower\_le\_upper} field, the statements remain mutually
consistent, but for any semantically correct instance reflecting the
GLW theorem, the axioms' convention would need to be swapped to match
the structure's docstring. This is a clerical (not mathematical) fix
and should be performed in a future commit.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Block independence vs.\ nested supremum]\label{rem:nested}
The helper \texttt{iIndepFun\_block\_sums} gives independence of
\emph{sums} over pairwise disjoint finite-index blocks from an i.i.d.\
sequence. This applies directly to increments $S_{n_{k+1}} - S_{n_k}$
(used in the LIL lower bound, sorries \#3--\#4).

It does \emph{not} apply directly to $\supnorm_{n_m}(\omega)$, which
depends on the whole prefix $(a_1,\ldots,a_{n_m})$. Independence of the
events $\{\supnorm_{n_m}\le\eps_m\sqrt{n_m}\}$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:SLE}
is obtained via KMT: couple $\supnorm_{n_m}/\sqrt{n_m}$ to a functional
of $B|_{[0,1]}$, decompose $B$ into independent increments, truncate
outside a block, and exploit that $\supnorm_{n_m}$ is asymptotically
determined by the most recent block's Brownian increment. This is
\emph{not} a direct instance of block independence: it is a
coupling-plus-truncation argument bundled into the KMT axiom, whose
assembly into the actual proof is the ~500-line BC content still
absent from \texttt{chojecki\_sparse\_lower\_envelope\_proof}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Endpoint reparametrization as a real theorem]
The sixth atomic axiom \texttt{endpoint\_reparametrization} is
elementary calculus and deserves elimination. A partial proof exists
(\texttt{Erdos524/EndpointReparametrization.lean}, not yet on
\texttt{main}) containing: the substitution
$u = -n\log x$, image computation $\{x\in(0,1]\} = \{u\in[0,\infty)\}$
and the matching formula
$a_k x^k \mapsto a_k e^{-uk/n}$. The final bridge into
$\supnorm_n$ (matching \texttt{randomPoly a n \(\omega\)} to the helper's
\texttt{Helpers.poly a'}) breaks on a \texttt{let}/\texttt{set}
opacity issue plus a small handful of \texttt{biSup} normalization
rewrites. Estimate to complete: 1--2 hours of careful Lean.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Sharp LIL tail via the $u^2 = 2s$ reparametrization]
The proof of \eqref{eq:PMF-sharp} in Lean foundered three times on
\texttt{maxHeartbeats} timeouts when tactics like \texttt{nlinarith},
\texttt{field\_simp}, and \texttt{ring} were applied directly to
expressions containing \texttt{Real.sqrt (2 * n * Real.log (Real.log n))}.
The key tactical move (lemma \texttt{margin\_absorbs\_sqrt\_core}) is to
introduce $u$ with a hypothesis $u^2 = 2s$ so that every inequality is
\emph{polynomial} in $(u, s, \delta)$; no \texttt{Real.sqrt} appears in
the arithmetic. This pattern is likely useful in other LIL-scale
formalizations.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The \texttt{answer(sorry)} elaborator]
The FormalConjectures framework provides \texttt{answer(\_)} as a
meta-syntactic placeholder; in \texttt{Prop} context with argument
\texttt{sorry}, it elaborates to \texttt{True}
(\texttt{FormalConjectures/Util/Answer.lean}). Thus
\texttt{erdos\_524 : answer(sorry) $\leftrightarrow$ RHS} reduces to
\texttt{True $\leftrightarrow$ RHS}, i.e.\ proving the right-hand side
directly. In our case the right-hand side asserts the existence of a
single function $f$ controlling both envelopes at the LIL scale, which
is now delivered by $f(n) := \sqrt{2n\log\log n}$ and the proven
$\limsup \supnorm_n/f(n) = 1$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Open problem: the exact constant (sorry~\#6)]
The final sorry concerns the almost-sure \emph{limit}
\[
  \lim_{m\to\infty}
    \frac{\log(\sqrt{n_m}/\supnorm_{n_m})}{(\log\log n_m)^{1/3}} = \alpha_*.
\]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:SLE} the limsup lies in $[\alpha_-,\alpha_+]$ almost
surely, but the sharp one-point asymptotic
$-\log\PP(\sup|Y|\le\eps) \sim c_*|\log\eps|^3$ on which
$\alpha_* = (1/(6c_*))^{1/3}$ would be computed is not known; it
remains an open problem in Gaussian-process small-ball theory
(Chojecki~2026 Remark~19). Closing this sorry therefore depends on
mathematical progress, not formalization effort.
\end{remark}
